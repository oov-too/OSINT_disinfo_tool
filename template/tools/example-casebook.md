# Example casebook: the invented cases the protocol documents use (platform phase 4, 2026-10-22)

Every example in the shipped protocol text (tools/schema.md and the protocol documents in tools/, and the brief templates) is drawn from the three cases below. They are invented. No person, organisation, place, event, document or quotation in them is real, and any resemblance to one is accidental. They exist so that a rule can be shown working on a row without quoting any project's corpus: a project's own examples live in its pack (`packs/<pack>/`), never in core text.

Use the names exactly as written here, so that an example in one document can be followed into another. Add a name only by adding it here first.

## Case 1: the Harrowmere spillway inquiry (a public inquiry)

On the night of 14 March 2017 the spillway of the Harrowmere reservoir failed in a storm and the valley town of Ostley Vale flooded. A public inquiry, the Harrowmere Inquiry, sat from 2019 to 2021 under Judge Imelda Corrin; its transcripts are cited by hearing day (`Day 12`) and its exhibits by number (`HI-114`).

| Who or what | Role in the case |
|---|---|
| The Vale Water Authority (the Authority) | owner of the reservoir; the agency under scrutiny; its 2018 review, *Report on the Harrowmere Event*, says no warning of cracking was received before 2017 |
| Desmond Harlowe | the Authority's chief executive 2012 to 2018; told the press in 2017 that "no warning was ever put in writing" |
| Gareth Lisle | the Authority's inspector; wrote the Lisle memo of 2 June 2015 warning of cracking in the spillway's left wall |
| Calloway Ashdown Civil (CAC) | the contractor that built the spillway in 2009 |
| Pernell Geotechnics | CAC's subcontractor for the foundations |
| Petra Vanecek | CAC's site engineer 2008 to 2012; firsthand on the 2009 concrete pour; gives evidence on Day 12 (5 November 2019) and talks about it again in two later interviews |
| Owen Thale | journalist; first published the Lisle memo in the *Ostley Gazette* on 9 October 2017 |
| "Heron" (pseudonym) | Thale's anonymous source inside the Authority |
| Ms Adair | counsel to the inquiry |
| *Harbour Talks* | an interview podcast (host Nell Ambrose) that interviewed Vanecek in March 2020 |
| *The Long Table* | a narrative podcast (host Rhea Castellan) whose 2022 series retold the case; a compiler |
| *Margin Notes* | an engineering blog by Ivo Strand, sceptical of the cover-up reading; an adverse source |
| The 2009 design certificate DC-0917 | CAC's certificate for the spillway |
| The site diary (exhibit HI-114) | CAC's daily site record for 2009 |
| Core sample C-14 | a concrete core taken from the left wall in 2011, tested, and later lost; the reference object of the example object register |
| The Harrowmere Waterworks Charter (1888) | the reservoir's founding charter in numbered clauses; the example canon text (the unit is the clause) |
| The 1888 commissioners' minutes | the record the Charter was drafted from |
| Alder Quayle | editor and translator of the annotated 1932 edition of the Charter, which renders its clauses in modern English; his glosses mostly name no source |
| Bryn Talley | author of the 1950 commentary printed inside later reprints of Quayle's edition |

Example eras: before 2009; 2009 to 2016; 2017 onward. Example circles: the engineering circle (CAC, Pernell, Vanecek), the Authority's circle (Harlowe, Lisle, the 2018 review), the press circle (Thale, *The Long Table*, *Margin Notes*). Example catalyst: the *Ostley Gazette* story of 9 October 2017 (a telling held before that date is "pre-catalyst held"). Example narratives: *negligence* (an ordinary engineering failure, nobody warned in time) and *concealment* (a warning was given and later managed out of the record).

Example codebook entry: in CAC's internal e-mail, "weather" stands for an inspection visit ("weather on Thursday"); a decode of a sentence by it is a lead naming the code, never a finding.

Example object register: reference object core sample C-14; criteria: core, labelled, dated, tested, chain-signed, missing.

Example disinformation lens from a text: the *Pellingham Crisis-Communications Manual* (2004, invented), whose techniques include the pre-emptive partial concession and flooding the record with true but irrelevant documents; each technique is attributed to the manual, the source that states it.

Example speaker ledger: Petra Vanecek, three appearances (inquiry Day 12, 2019-11-05; *Harbour Talks*, 2020-03-18; *The Long Table* episode 4, 2022-06-02).

Example subjects of a pack built on this case: `infrastructure-safety`, `public-accountability`; audited subject: `infrastructure-safety`. Example topic codes: `design-and-construction`, `inspection-and-warnings`, `decision-makers`, `money-and-contracts`, `other`.

## Case 2: Corvane Holdings (a corporate record)

Corvane Holdings is a logistics group. Its 2014 internal audit found transfers routed through an arrangement called Project Larkspur. Mara Quillon, its former head of treasury, raised the transfers internally in 2014 and with the regulator, the Markets Standards Board, in 2016. In 2019 a set of internal files, the Corvane files, was leaked to the press by an anonymous source.

| Who or what | Role in the case |
|---|---|
| Mara Quillon | former head of treasury; firsthand on the 2014 transfers |
| Anton Vesk | Corvane's finance director 2011 to 2016 |
| Brightwell Fenn LLP | Corvane's external auditor |
| The Markets Standards Board (the Board) | the regulator; an agency party |
| The board minutes of 3 April 2014 | the minutes at which Larkspur was approved |
| The Larkspur spreadsheet | the transfer schedule, held in the leaked files |
| The Corvane files | the 2019 leak (a leaked document is hearsay with the leaker as its root until an official release matches it) |

## Case 3: the Velmora and Kestria timber dispute (a trade dispute)

In 2011 the Republic of Velmora imposed an export levy on hardwood. Kestria complained to the Joint Trade Panel, which heard the case from 2012 to 2014. Kestria's envoy Anselm Dorr led its case; Velmora's trade minister was Ilse Morrow. In 2015 a set of diplomatic cables, the Dorr cables, was leaked.

| Who or what | Role in the case |
|---|---|
| Velmora, Kestria | states (polities) |
| The Timber Exporters' League | a non-state actor lobbying for the levy |
| Greenhollow Trading | a company the cables call "a front for the League" (an order-of-battle relation) |
| The Joint Trade Panel | the tribunal; its rulings are public record |
| The Dorr cables | a leak, hearsay until an official release matches it |

Example referent object of an inquiry into the dispute: the regional timber market; example priority intelligence requirement (PIR): "who set the levy's rate and on whose advice", with its essential elements of information (date, office, instrument).
