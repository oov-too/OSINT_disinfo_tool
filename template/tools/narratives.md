# Narratives: the cross-source narrative mode and its agent workflow (v0.3, 2026-10-09)

Status: design agreed with the owner on 2026-10-08 (section 8 records the decisions); pilot running. The owner's base reading: the material comes from one interconnected milieu whose main compilers (the active pack's compilers, {{pack.compilers}}; in the casebook's terms, a narrative podcast such as *The Long Table*) have each built a narrative that fits their own model, and the database's job is to find patterns beneath them, in particular what people who cannot speak hint at by what they omit, how they speak around it, and what they say offhand. The owner's brief (2026-10-08): find coherent narratives across speakers, titles, sources and claims; narratives with the fewest or least impactful contradictions, and alternative narratives that take a contradiction as the truth and rebuild cogency from that node forward; take timing into account (what happens at the same time in different domains); use the speaker's openness, and who or what each narrative benefits or hurts, with affiliations; primary scope the active pack's subjects from its scope start year onward ({{pack.subjects}}, {{pack.eras}}), on the understanding that it all connects.

## 1. What a Narrative is, and is not

A **Narrative** is a derived research page (research/narrative-<slug>.md, flat under research/ because build_db.py globs research/*.md only; page kind `narrative`) that states one thesis as a dated causal story: an ordered set of **nodes** (event clusters or claim clusters) spanning at least two **lanes** (section 3), each node carrying its independent roots, and an explicit stance on every contradiction the story touches. It is scored (section 6), never ranked by a single number.

Two modes:
- **Mainline**: at every contradiction it touches, the narrative takes the side that keeps the weighted contradiction load lowest for the coverage it achieves.
- **Fork**: a narrative born at one named contradiction (its **fork node**). It takes the losing telling as true and rebuilds the story forward from that node: which later nodes change, which rows that were anomalies ("new", unexplained, closed-subtle) now fit, which rows that fitted now contradict. A fork is reported against its parent as a change in load and a change in coverage, not as a verdict.

It differs from a **through-line** (research/through-lines-2026-09-26.md: a claim cluster and its carriers) in having causal order and dates across lanes. It differs from an **investigation** (tools/investigation.md: adjudication between explanations already on the table) in proposing the story. It differs from a **synthesis entry** (research/synthesis-*.md) in being structured, scored and comparable, with a parsable node table. It is a derived page: it holds no claims of its own, loads into notes.db as a page plus three structured tables (section 7), and its findings that outlive it go where findings live (leads, relations, the contradictions page).

Two standing rules carry over unchanged. The investigation standard (investigation.md section 2.2): state the strongest alternative at once, take deflationary readings seriously, hedge inference in the author's words, refuse tests no outsider can run, draw the chain. And the house rules on custody: independence is counted in chain roots, not mouths (through-lines "owner's rule"); retellings and host loops collapse; echoes (tools/echoes.py) collapse further; an agency's contradiction of a witness is a lead, not a correction (the agency rule: the divergence rule's agency clause, tools/schema.md section 10); the deflationary milieu narrative (the base reading's milieu telling its own story) is itself a candidate and may win.

## 3. Lanes (the domains for timing)

Mechanical assignment from topic code, note subject and event wording (tools/narrative_pack.py states its rules); agents may reassign a node and say why.

The lanes' domain content (what each lane is called in a pack, which topic codes, subjects and tags feed it) is the active pack's ({{pack.narratives}}: the pack's briefs/narrative-lanes.md; the topic codes are {{pack.topics}}). The table gives the form: the lane letters, what kind of event each lane holds, and the kind of mechanical source.

| Lane | What goes in | Mechanical source |
|---|---|---|
| A. Main subject, hidden | what the parties under scrutiny did out of public view: programmes, custody of objects and records, retrievals, gatekeepers | the pack's lane-A topic codes in notes on the pack's main subject |
| B. Main subject, public | hearings, laws, the official bodies' statements, press, disclosures, documentaries, the public figures' moves | notes of kind hearing; the pack's lane-B topic codes in main-subject notes; the pack's lane-B tags (the official bodies, the laws, hearing) |
| C. Second subject, institutional | the programmes, institutions and operators of the pack's second subject | the pack's lane-C topic codes in programme notes |
| D. Second subject, experience | firsthand experience, relayed voices, injuries, consent | the pack's lane-D topic codes; relayed-voice rows ({{pack.channelled_clause}}) |
| E. Money and contracts | funding flows, contracts, internal research funds, grants, misappropriation, budgets | the pack's money topic code; the pack's contractor topic code in rows about money |
| H. Organisations | founding, renaming, staffing, moves and dissolution of agencies, offices, companies, foundations, societies and programs as bodies | lifecycle verbs on named bodies; relations founder, directs, member-of, employer with dates |
| F. Geopolitics | wars, elections, treaties, adversary programs | the pack's geopolitics topic code; dated public events |
| G. Antecedents | old texts, religion, prehistory, material before the pack's scope start | the pack's antecedents topic code and every note before the scope start ({{pack.eras}}) |

Illustration in the casebook's terms (tools/example-casebook.md; invented): lane A holds the Lisle memo of 2 June 2015 and what the Authority did with it; lane B the inquiry's hearing days and the *Ostley Gazette* story of 9 October 2017; lane E the 2009 contract and Pernell Geotechnics' subcontract (topic `money-and-contracts`); lane H the lifecycles of the Authority, Calloway Ashdown Civil and Pernell Geotechnics.

Scope rule: nodes before the pack's scope start year ({{pack.eras}}) enter only as lane G antecedents cited by a carrier inside the scope (in the casebook's terms, the Harrowmere Waterworks Charter of 1888 where a carrier invokes it). A **dense window** is a span (year, or quarter from the pack's quarter-start year, {{pack.eras}}) in which three or more lanes have dated events; the pack lists them, and every narrative must say what the other lanes were doing in each dense window it crosses.

## 4. The pack (phase 0), file by file

- **synchrony-index.md** (every dense window listed, no cap: change 37) and **synchrony/<era>.md**: a year-by-lane grid from the pack's scope start year to the present (quarters from the pack's quarter-start year; both {{pack.eras}}). Each cell lists event clusters (from the era pages' clusters where they exist, else timeline rows) as `cluster: carriers (independent roots) [agreement kind]`. Then the dense windows ranked.
- **contradictions.md**: every contradiction node, ranked by a provisional impact score. Sources: timeline rows with agreement kind contradicts or drifts (and their `[div: dir, core, stab, indep, timing, path, party]` tags, already on 1,100 rows), cross-checks with verdict contradicts, leads of kind divergence, the 121 clusters on the contradictions page. Provisional impact = core (1.0) or periphery (0.3) × independent parties (1.0) or not (0.4) × 1 + 0.1 per further row in the same event cluster, + 0.5 if a party is an agency or official. This is a seed ranking only; the real impact is computed per narrative (section 6).
- **speakers.md**: one sheet per carrier with 20 or more claims rows: recording span; source-class mix; openness counts per topic (audit_rows, openness-dataset.json); affiliations with dates (relations employer, member-of, funder, founder, directs, owner, operator); adversaries and critics; divergence tally (divergence-patterns.md); echo overlaps (tools/echoes.py); the carriers they relay and who relays them (chains).
- **signals.md** (the hints layer, owner 2026-10-08): per carrier, the walls and what is said near them: stated declines and closed-subtle rows with their grounds; claims rows the speaker closes or partials; and the rows the same speaker volunteers (move n/a) or answers openly on the topics they close elsewhere, listed as 'near the wall'. Also the leads of kind absence. Builders read omissions, speaking-around and offhand remarks as signal candidates, recorded in the Nodes table with Role `signal` and never promoted to a claim.
- **seeds.md**: candidate theses with their terms, drawn from the through-lines crossings table, the synthesis entries, leads of kind thread, and the dense windows. The owner edits this list before phase 2.

## 5. The narrative page (schema)

Metadata table, keys in this order: Kind (`narrative`), Mode (`mainline` or `fork`), Parent (fork only: the parent page and its fork node number), Thesis (one sentence), Alternative (the strongest rival, one sentence), Span, Lanes, Built, Author (the task label), Status, Related.

Sections, headings exact:

`## Thesis` prose: the story in one paragraph; the alternative stated at once; the inherited caveats declared once.

`## Nodes` `| # | Date | Lane | Node | Carriers (independent roots) | Rows | Basis | Depends on | Role |`
Basis (owner, 2026-10-25, from the pilot's truth check: five nodes the courts confirmed were carried by a witness's recollection or an auditor's inference where the court had the document): the strongest evidence class among the node's cited rows, one word: `document` (a held document's own words: a `read` row whose work is held, or a document note's own `host` row), `record` (a document attested but not held: read through counsel, a hearing's description of a paper, a `read` row whose work is cited and not held), `recollection` (a witness's `firsthand` memory of events), `inference` (an auditor's, analyst's or counsel's reasoning), `press` (a press report with no document behind it). A node whose strongest basis is recollection, inference or press is thin: the cell reads `recollection; thin`, and `## Leads returned` carries, for every thin node, the document that would settle it and where it would be found. The score line counts them: `thin nodes N of N`. A row that argues against the node (the other side) never raises its Basis: it belongs in the Contradiction ledger, and only the rows the node rests on count.
Role: cause, enabler, effect, cover, signal, anomaly-absorbed. Depends on: node numbers; this column is what makes impact computable (section 6). Rows: `note:table:n` citations with class and chain as the through-lines page writes them.

`## Contradiction ledger` `| # | Node | Contradiction | Side taken | Other side | Weight | Downstream | Why survivable | Fork | Flare |`
Flare (owner, 2026-10-08): a high-impact contradiction whose party is inside a program, or an agency, may be misdirection rather than error; the cell says `possible flare: <why>` or `no`, and the investigation standard's disinformation reading (investigation.md section 2.1 item 12) is applied to it.
Weight per section 6. Downstream: the nodes that would change if the other side were true (the forward closure of Depends on). Fork: `candidate`, `spawned: <slug>`, or `no: <reason>`.

`## Synchrony` `| Window | This narrative | Other lanes at the same time | Reading |` one row per dense window crossed.

`## Carriers` `| Carrier | Root or relay | Openness on this narrative's topics | Affiliations (dated) | Echo overlap | Note |`

`## Interests` `| Party | Benefits or is hurt | Through which nodes | Affiliated carriers | Note |` recorded symmetrically for agencies, companies, witnesses and hosts (decision 2 below).

`## Objections` written by the phase 4 agent: independence collapses, interest alignment, closures the narrative fails to predict, contradictions it did not list, the deflationary reading.

`## Score card` ending with one line in this exact form (v0.3, 2026-10-08):
`Narrative scores: clusters N, rows N, roots N, circle roots N, reading roots N, contradictions N, load X.X, max downstream N, closures predicted N of N, unaffiliated roots N of N, survive under rival N of N, tests N, thin nodes N of N.` (thin nodes from 2026-10-25; a page without the axis is read as before)
Roots counts the independent roots of the events as flagged (every firsthand carrier a root, affiliation on the interest axis); circle roots counts them again after circle collapse, one root per circle, where a circle is a set of carriers joined by a dated employer, patron, company or programme edge in the relations register at the time of the telling (change 28, owner's option 3); the page states its circle definition and lists its circles once, in the Score card; reading roots counts the roots that carry the page's reading of them (change 26). Survive under rival counts the nodes that stand unchanged under the Alternative, and the Tests section puts the discriminating tests first (change 35). Pilot pages written before v0.2 carry the shorter line; tools/narratives.py reads both.

`## Forks` the fork candidates and spawned forks.

`## Tests` finite tests with a place to look, the investigation standard.

`## Leads returned` rows in the leads-register format, kind thread, inference or divergence.

## 5a. The synchrony page (schema)

Metadata keys: Kind (`narrative-synchrony`), Era, Built, Author (task label), Sources, Rows considered, Status, Related. Sections, headings exact (trimmed 2026-10-09: the pilot's builders used the co-movements, contradictions, signals and 'for the builders' tables and little else, and the phase cost 3.4 million tokens): `## Lanes in this era` (one paragraph per lane A to H, the roots and the absences, no row lists); `## Windows` (dense windows only, three or more lanes) `| Window | Lanes active | What moves | Carriers (roots) | Rows | Reading |`; `## Co-movements` `| # | Window(s) | Lanes | What moves together | Independent roots | Rows | Reading | Bears on seeds |`; `## Contradictions` `| # | Pack rank | Window | Node | Side A (rows) | Side B (rows) | Parties | Insider or agency | Provisional impact | Flare | Bears on seeds |`; `## Signals` `| # | Carrier | Window | Wall (rows, ground) | Near the wall (rows) | Reading |`; `## Organisations` `| Body | Event | Window | Rows | Roots |`; `## For the builders` (a subsection per seed); `## Leads returned`; `## Relations returned`.

## 6. Scoring (five axes, no composite)

- **Coverage**: clusters and rows explained; independent roots after chain, host-loop and echo collapse, given twice: as flagged (each firsthand carrier a root) and after circle collapse (one root per circle; the circle rule, owner 2026-10-08: both figures always, the circle definition stated once per page, never collapse alone and never flag alone). A narrative that claims little has few contradictions; coverage is what it must be read against.
- **Contradiction load**: Σ weight over the ledger, weight = core (1.0) or periphery (0.3) × independent parties (1.0) or same line (0.4) × (1 + downstream count ÷ node count). Downstream is the narrative's own dependency closure, so the same contradiction weighs more in a story that leans on it.
- **Openness fit**: for each carrier, the topics they close on (closed-outright, closed-subtle, stated declines); the narrative says which closures it predicts (in the casebook's terms: a *concealment* story predicts `inspection-and-warnings` closed among the Authority's carriers; a story of a contractor's cut corners predicts `design-and-construction` closed among Calloway Ashdown Civil's). Score: predicted ÷ total, where the wall set excludes walls whose stated ground is ordinary and not the story's: classification, pre-publication review, a pending investigation, or protecting a named person (owner, 2026-10-08, change 27; the pack's names for these grounds are in {{pack.basis_vocabulary}}). A promise or confidence is not an ordinary ground: it is a cheap out that makes the speaker look trustworthy, so those walls stay in the set. The page states how the wall set was chosen and how many walls the exclusion removed. Rule (owner, 2026-10-08): openness is not truth. Open speakers may be wrong or serving an agenda; closed speakers may want the truth out and be unable to say it. A closure is a wall, not a denial, and never evidence for the content behind it; a channelled voice's zero declines is unfalsifiability, not openness (the pack's channelled-class clause, {{pack.channelled_clause}}).
- **Interest alignment**: roots unaffiliated with any party the narrative benefits, over all roots. Affiliation flags; it never excludes a carrier. The dated affiliation rule is applied evenly to every root (change 29): three classes, unaffiliated, affiliated and adverse, affiliated and not adverse; adversity is read from the carrier's own rows (what they say against the party), not from the relations register's adversary and critic edges; unnamed roots are counted in neither column and reported apart.
- **Tests**: how many finite tests the page names with a place to look. A story with none is not yet a narrative.

Flares: a contradiction marked `possible flare` is also written to the leads register (kind divergence) with the parties and the test that would separate error from misdirection. The Flare cell itself names that test, one an outsider can run, or says `no separating test exists` (change 31); a record that merely settles the fact is not a flare test.

Fork rules (changes 32 to 34): a fork first carries its parent attacker's corrections (broken nodes repaired or dropped, unsupported edges removed) before rebuilding, and lists them under `### Carried from the parent's attacker`; it names one flare reading at the fork node (misdirection or error) and carries it through every node; it reports its deltas against the parent's attacker-corrected score line and graph, with any graph corrections on their own line of the Deltas table.

Revision rule (2026-10-09; proposal 25 adopted the same day): a reviser verifies an objection's own rows before adopting it; an objection whose rows do not support it is declined with the rows named, never adopted (the second attack pass found two adopted objections had re-broken rows: row 13 of mainline 1, node 47 of mainline 2; in this document's run records, mainline 1 to mainline 6 and fork 1a and the like stand for the pack's narrative pages in seed order, {{pack.narratives}}, the labels tools/adoption_check.md uses). The Revision table carries `Rows read` and `Leads checked` for every objection; an adopted objection with an empty Rows read cell fails the check.

Builder rule (change 30): before writing the ledger, the builder lists every timeline row among its own citations whose agreement kind is contradicts or drifts, every cross-check and divergence lead on its nodes, and gives each a ledger row or a stated reason for leaving it out.

Fork reporting: Δ load, Δ coverage, anomalies absorbed (rows the parent left as `new` or unexplained that the fork places), nodes broken. Forks are ranked by anomalies absorbed and by the discriminating tests they name, never by load (2026-10-09: no pilot fork lowered load; their value was elsewhere); load per cluster (tools/narratives.py prints it) is reported beside load so a page that claims little does not look clean.

Closures predicted retired from the score line (owner, 2026-10-09, proposal 24): after the re-attack it separated no reading from its rival on any page (register section 5). The closures table stays in Carriers with the subset of walls a reading predicts and its rival does not, as a note, never a score. Score line v0.4: `Narrative scores: clusters N, rows N, roots N, circle roots N, reading roots N, contradictions N, load X.X, max downstream N, unaffiliated roots N of N, survive under rival N of N, tests N.`; --check refuses a v0.3 line unless --v03-ok.

Edge rule (proposal 27): every Depends-on edge needs a row that joins the two nodes; an edge the page reads but no row supports is marked a reading edge, left out of the downstream closure, and counted on the score card.

Fork anomalies (proposal 28): anomalies absorbed are counted by independent root cluster, each marked discriminating (the parent cannot place it) or fitting both; forks rank on the discriminating count and their tests. Re-measurement (proposal 31): when a parent is revised its forks are re-measured against the new attacker line at the next revise step, and carry `dated:` in their Deltas until then.

Tests already run (proposal 32): a test the held rows answer is reported as run, with its branch, under `### Tests already run`, never among the open tests.

Synchrony citation form (proposal 30): `[synchrony <era> <table> <row>]`, one form on every page.

The rival rule (2026-10-09): each rival the pages name (the milieu, the hidden history, agency management) has one sheet, research/narrative-rival-<slug>.md; a page's Thesis cites the sheet for its Alternative; an attacker's deflationary reading starts from the sheet and adds only rows under its `## Additions`; no page re-derives a rival.

## 7. Database and tools

- build_db.py: page kind `narrative`; tables `narratives` (page, mode, parent, fork_node, thesis, alternative, span, lanes, scores...), `narrative_nodes` (page, n, date, lane, node, carriers, rows, depends_on, role, basis; basis empty on a page without the column), `narrative_contradictions` (page, n, node, contradiction, side, other, weight, downstream, survivable, fork).
- `tools/narratives.py`: computes downstream closures from Depends on, recomputes the score line, lists the family tree, and tallies which contradiction nodes recur across narratives by their cited rows and leads, cross-mainline recurrences reported apart from rows a fork inherits (change 24); citations are keyed with their kind, timeline, claims or audit (change 25).
- `tools/narratives.py --sweep <page> --out <file>`: the contradiction sweep (change 30) as a file for the author; `--interests <page> --out <file>` (or `--interests - --names "A|B|C"`): the Interests seed from the relations register; `--audit <page> --out <file>`: the attacker's mechanical tests, ending with the basis audit (section 6 of its output: per node the class its rows carry, inferred from each row's Source class and chain and its note's kind, beside the page's Basis cell, with a mismatch flag and the thin count against the score line; test 9 starts there); `--check <page>`: the v0.3 form (exit 1 on failure), and on a page with a Basis column a class in every cell and a `thin nodes N of N` axis that agrees with the cells. The score line parser reads the axis when it is there and the older lines as before.
- `tools/narrative_unused.py <page> --out <file>` (`ledger narrate unused`, 2026-10-25; section 8e): the unused-rows check. For every node it gathers the names (the Carriers cell, the speakers of the node's rows, the Carriers table, the capitalised names in the node text), the distinctive terms (every word the node shares with its own rows, and the rarest others) and the topics of its rows, and the window (the Date cell's years, widened a year each side for `about`, then `--near` years); it searches the claims, timeline and audit rows of every note for the rows cited nowhere on the page (any citation in any section, short references bound to the note last cited) whose matches together would turn up by chance in at most two held rows (`--chance`; an earlier row up to `--reach-back` years before the window, or an undated one, at 0.4, `--chance-out`). It ranks them: an earlier date than the node's start, a held document (a `read` row whose work is held, a document note's own row), the other side (a speaker the node's Carriers do not name; counsel and chairs are no side), the rest; and prints per node the head line (names, terms, topics, window, what was cut) and `| Row | Date | Speaker | Class | What it says | Why it bears |`, `--cap` rows (12) with the rest indexed by note under the table. `--node N` for one node; `--json` for a script. The author reads every row shown; exit 2 on a page with no Nodes table. Outputs go to the agent's wip folder; an agent reads them by shell and never passes them through its reply.
- `tools/narrative_digest.py <seed>`: the builder digest (wip/narratives-1008/digests/<seed>.md).
- `tools/report_check.py <report.json> --form <narrative|fork|attack|revise|synchrony|register|intake-document|intake-video>`: the report validator every agent runs before replying (blocked_passages required).
- `tools/narrative_pack.py`: phase 0. Rebuilt after phase 1 on 2026-10-08: bare surnames resolve through each note's Guest, Host and Speakers fields before the global merge; the insider mark excludes carriers who host three or more notes; lane H needs a named body as well as a lifecycle verb.
- `tools/narrative_apply.py <pages>`: applies a page's `## Leads returned` and `## Relations returned` tables to the registers (through tools/add_leads.py; relations with the next number and the integrate_report dedupe key) and fills the page's lead numbers in place. Run after every phase, then `python3 tools/build_db.py --report`.
- Briefs, one per phase, under wip/narratives-<date>/: brief-synchrony.md, brief-builder.md, brief-fork.md, brief-attack.md, brief-register.md. Every brief carries the blocked-output paragraph (tools/filter-blocks.md section 5) and the checkpoint rule.
- `tools/build_narrative_viewer.py` builds the viewer page research/figures/narratives-viewer.html from the pages, the register and the pack (template tools/narrative_viewer_template.html); published as a private artifact only where the project's setting allows it (setup stage 7); rebuild, and republish to the same URL, after every phase.
- build_db.py loads the narrative pages into `narratives`, `narrative_nodes` and `narrative_contradictions` through tools/narratives.py (`downstream_computed` is the closure from Depends on; the page's own Downstream cell is kept beside it).

## 8b. The WIP and blocked-output policies in this mode
Every agent follows tools/agent-wip.md (progress.md from the first minute, the deliverable written section by section, report.json built incrementally, current-unit.md before every large write, resume from wip never from scratch) and tools/filter-blocks.md (the standing paragraph in every brief; the orchestrator layer: guarded-intake, `python3 tools/workflow_health.py <run> --runlog` on every hand-back, bisect-block after a repeat death). Tool outputs (digest, sweep, interests, audit) are files the agent reads; nothing fetched or generated passes through an agent's reply. The orchestrator keeps wip/narratives-<date>/RESUME.md current (step table, run ids, args files) and copies tool-feedback artefacts (lane reassignments, sweep decisions) to research/tasks/tool-feedback/<run>/ before deleting a task's wip folder.

## 8c. The staged form (owner, 2026-10-09; token points 1 to 4 and 6)

Measured on a run-2 reviser: 315 turns, 1.46M tokens read once, 42k generated, 130M re-read from cache (the whole context on every turn). Reading everything is cheap; carrying it through hundreds of turns is the cost. So: (1) a page task is split into short-lived agents with small contexts, run by tools/workflows/staged-page.js from wip/narratives-1009/phases/ briefs: revise = check (objections against their rows) -> sweep (dispositions in chunks of about 300 rows, in parallel) -> write; attack = tests A and B in parallel -> write; build = candidates (reads the digest in full in an otherwise empty context) -> draft -> sweep -> finish. Each phase hands the next a file in wip/<task>/. (2) Receipts stay off the page: sweep dispositions live in wip/<task>/sweep-dispositions-*.tsv (archived), and `--slim` moves any on-page list to research/tasks/tool-feedback/receipts/, leaving a count line. (3) Earlier Objections and Revision passes move to research/narrative-history/<stem>.md after each attack (`--slim`); the page keeps the latest of each and every attacker line. (4) Lookups are batched (`--rows <file>`) and reads are of sections (`--section`), never whole pages; a row not yet known by reference is found with `python3 tools/rows_search.py "<query>" [--note <glob>] [--table claims|timeline|audit]` (full-text search over the row tables, returning citations with cells; tools/rows_search.md, 2026-10-15) before any grep of the markdown or read of a note. (6) The attacker gets a row-level diff since the last snapshot (tools/narrative_snapshot.py before a revise step; `--diff` at attack launch) and spends its judgement on what changed while the audit covers the whole page. Every row is still read; nothing is omitted. Per-phase models are allowed in staged-page.js (`model` on a phase). Owner ruling 2026-10-14, after two side-by-sides (sweep dispositions: 509 of 582 identical, 13 omissions of the kind the rules forbid; build finish: substance matched, tests and leads for new flares not written): Opus throughout this workflow; the savings are not worth the risk to the result.

## 8d. First-utterance tests (owner, 2026-10-14; register form 2026-10-14)

The discriminating tests of both runs were the order of first utterance and dated documents no carrier could have supplied, so the mode carries them as a corpus-level register that intake maintains and the narrative process reads (owner: "update first utterance with intake"). The pack's catalysts page ({{pack.catalysts}}) lists the public events a later telling could have been built from (29 rows at the ruling; `tools/first_utterance.py --catalysts` checks each is held as a dated timeline row). research/first-utterance-register.md holds one row per claim cluster (ID FU-n, permanent): the cited rows, the earliest held telling (recomputed from notes.db by `--refresh`), the earliest known public telling (written by intake through the report's `first_telling_rows` and `--apply`, or by hand from research/early-tellings-review.md as `candidate: ...` until held), the catalyst before the earliest held telling, and the narrative nodes that join the cluster. `--build` seeds clusters from the pages' nodes and only adds. ## 8e. The basis audit and the unused-rows check (owner, 2026-10-25)

Two methods from the Post Office pilot's truth check against the held judgments (ledger-pilot/wip/truth-check/VERDICT.md: no node contradicted; five nodes the courts confirmed were carried by a recollection or an inference where the court had the document; one avoidable date miss sat in a held row the page never cited).

1. **The basis audit.** Every node carries its Basis (section 5): the strongest evidence class among its rows. A thin node (recollection, inference, press) is not struck, since the page states what the held rows let it state; it is marked, counted on the score line (`thin nodes N of N`), and given a lead naming the document that would settle it. The builder fills the column from the rows' class and chain; the attacker checks it (test 9) and objects where a cause or enabler node is thin, or where a node's basis is overstated (a recollection written as a record); the reviser repairs a thin node where a held row or an unused row supplies the document.
2. **The unused-rows check.** `ledger narrate unused <page>` (tools/narrative_unused.py) lists, for every node, the held claims and timeline rows that match the node's names, dates and terms and are cited nowhere on the page, ranked by how far they would move the node (an earlier date, a document behind a recollection, the other side). The builder runs it before the Score card and reads every listed row, citing it or dismissing it under `### Rows read and left out` with a reason; the attacker runs it again (test 10) and objects where an unused row moves a node's date or basis. No silent caps: the report states what it searched and what it cut.

Both apply to every page from 2026-10-25, in the first project and in any pack; pages built before carry no Basis column until retrofitted (a backfill pass per page, owner's call), and the parser reads them as before.

## 9. Pilot record (2026-10-08)

Run as five guarded-intake workflows, 20 Opus agents, no deaths, no blocked passages: 7 synchrony pages (148,000 words; about 860 of 6,768 pack rows reassigned out of the mechanical lanes), 3 mainlines (mainlines 1, 2 and 3), 3 forks (fork 1a at mainline 1 row 13, a fork on a programme's purpose: the programme as a custody vehicle; fork 1b at mainline 1 row 17, a fork on who briefed a public figure: insiders briefing him; fork 3a at mainline 3 row 37, a fork on a dated exercise: the exercise as an agency's hypothetical), 6 attackers, 1 register. Registers: leads 2039 to 2289 and relations 9506 to 9664 added through tools/narrative_apply.py and tools/add_leads.py.

What the pilot showed about the method:
- Attackers change the picture: every page's load rose (mainline 1, 18.2 to 30.4; mainline 3, 34.2 to 43.1; the programme-purpose fork 21.6 to 40.0) once the contradictions the page touched but did not list were weighed; openness fit fell on every page, because authors counted walls the story absorbs as walls it predicts. The two-line score card (author and attacker) is kept as the standard.
- No fork lowered the load. Forks earn their place by anomalies absorbed and by naming what would flip them (the programme-purpose fork's timing problem: the only dated agency account puts the custody attempt around 2009 to 2010; the briefing fork: every held route runs through the public figure himself; the exercise fork collapses to its parent if the office's account is misdirection).
- The signals layer works but needs the attacker: hints held (one witness's offhand phrase about a network of people, the oath boundary another witness kept on a named programme, a third witness's offhand metaphor), and the attackers struck readings that outran their rows.
- Pack rules that misfired, fixed the same day: bare surnames merged into the wrong full name (resolve through the note's Guest field); the insider mark caught hosts and authors (exclude carriers hosting three or more notes; authors and people telling their own experience still slip through, so builders judge it); lane H caught non-lifecycle rows (now needs a named body); 2026-Q3's density is inflated by one compiler's posting metadata (a batch of forum posts; not fixed; the synchrony page lists the rows).
- tools/narratives.py miscounted Downstream cells of the form "11 (nodes 26, 27, ...)" until mainline 2's attacker caught it; mainline 2's author's counts were right.
- The next investigations are the contradictions every narrative had to take a side on (register section 3): what a programme was for, who briefed a public figure, a dated exercise, a second programme's end, a proposed programme's fate.
- The register (research/narrative-register.md, 13,600 words) lists 11 full-run items, 12 investigations and 14 proposed protocol changes in its `## Next` section; the protocol changes are not adopted until the owner rules on them. The task wip folders were deleted after their reports were applied (agent-wip rule 2); the pack and briefs stay under wip/narratives-1008/ for the full run.
