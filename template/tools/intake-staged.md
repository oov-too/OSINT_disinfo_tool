# The staged intake (2026-10-16)

An intake run as short-lived agents with small contexts, in the manner of the narratives' staged form (tools/narratives.md section 8c; tools/workflows/staged-page.js), with two script gates between the phases so that nothing read is lost on the way to the note. Built on the measurement below; the owner's rule for it: no coverage lost (every unit read in full, every row and quotation as accurate as the single-agent form made them), accuracy first, Opus agents at high effort, no work omitted to save tokens.

Pieces: `tools/workflows/staged-intake.js` (the workflow), `tools/intake_gate.py` (the two gates), `python3 ledger.py quote` (tools/ledger/quote.py: the source's words by pointer, for the compact findings form of section 3, and by word with `--find "<a few of the words>"`, which prints the sentence or caption line that holds them with its pointer), `wip/intake-staged/phases/` (README, read.md, write.md, write-unit.md, check.md, finish.md: the phase briefs), `tools/agent_usage.py` (the measure), this page. The standing briefs are unchanged and still bind every phase: wip/intake-1008/brief-document.next.md, wip/adverse-1014/brief-adverse.md, wip/intake-1008/COMMON.md, tools/schema.md, tools/agent-wip.md, tools/filter-blocks.md.

## 1. Why: the measured baseline

Before the staged form, one Opus agent per source ran from reading to report under `tools/workflows/guarded-intake.js`, the fan-out guard (resume from wip on a death, bisect-block after the last resume); that single-agent form is not shipped. Everything the agent reads stays in its context for every later turn, so the cost is the context carried through hundreds of turns, not the reading. Four comparable runs on 2026-10-15 (pair 1, two batches of one researcher's blog posts, the old way; pair 2, two batches of a second researcher's blog posts, with the pre-pass tools of that day: pre-cut units, skeleton, context pack, timeline candidates, note_fill, rows_search), measured by `python3 tools/agent_usage.py <run>` from the transcript jsonl (section 7 gives the columns):

## 2. The shape

```
orchestrator: chunk_source -> note_skeleton -> context_pack -> timeline_prepass -> TASK.md, prompt.md
   |
   v
read   one agent, or one per unit range over about 60,000 words; every unit in manifest order; findings/NNN.md per unit,
   |   the compact form (pointers, key words, quotation anchors; within the item allowance, section 3)
   |   gate 1: python3 tools/intake_gate.py --findings --wip wip/<task> --mark   (readers resumed on the failing units)
   v
write A  a fresh agent: the metadata table, the claims ledger and the forthrightness audit from findings/, skeleton.md
   |     (those sections), context.md sections 1 to 3; the schema's sections 1 to 3 and the standing rules by line range;
   |     the source never whole, its words pulled span by span with python3 ledger.py quote; parts of 3,000 words
   |     gate 2: python3 tools/intake_gate.py --note <note> --wip wip/<task>      (writer A resumed with the report)
   v
write B  a fresh agent: every other section from findings/, skeleton.md, timeline-candidates.md, context.md sections 1, 3 to 5,
   |     ledger-notes.md; the finished ledger by line search, never in full; the schema's sections 4 to 12 by line range
   |     gate 2 again                                                             (writer B resumed with the report)
   v
finish a fresh agent: build_db (check.db), onboard_check, note_fill, cross-checks read on both sides, report_check --pre-sample,
   |   report.json; gate 2 again                                                (the finisher resumed with the report)
   v
sample every intake (2026-10-25): python3 ledger.py sample draw <note> --wip wip/<task> --n 20 (a script: twenty claims rows by
   |   seed, spread over the units, each beside its span); one Opus reader under phases/sample.md (five judgements per row,
   |   sample-reading.md and .tsv); python3 ledger.py sample tally (a script) -> report.json sample_reading. Faults of meaning
   |   at the pack's [intake] sample_threshold (default 3) and no check phase run: the check over the whole ledger, gate 2, a
   |   redraw (round 2, a new seed) read once more, both tallies kept (sample_reading.redraw); the check already run: flagged
   v
report JSON -> the orchestrator integrates as before (integrate_report.py, blocked_passages.py register, source_balance, divergences)
```

Every agent is guarded as in guarded-intake.js: a null result (the API refused its output, or a terminal error) resumes it from its wip folder up to `maxResumes` (default 2), and a death after the last resume runs `bisect-block` over `wip/<task>/current-unit.md` (tools/filter-blocks.md sections 6 and 7). The reader copies the unit in hand there; the writer the findings file it is rowing; the finisher the onboard report.

What the split buys. The reader's context is the briefs, the schema's four sections and one unit at a time; it writes its findings as a digest, within the item allowance (section 3). Each writer's context is the sections of the schema and the briefs its part needs (by line range, never in full), the findings, the part of the skeleton and the context pack its sections use, the spans it pulls by pointer for the rows that need the source's words, and the parts it writes; writer B carries the finished ledger only by line search. A writer carries the source at most once, span by span, never whole and never twice, and the standing reading once per call rather than the whole of it. The finisher's context is the note's rows, the onboard report, the rows elsewhere it reads and any span it pulls to check a row. None of them carries what the others needed.

What the first measurement found (2026-10-22/23, the round trip of nine sources in a copy of the project; wip/platform-phase6a/REPORT.md sections 7 and 8). The design above assumed findings of about a tenth of the unit's words; the form of 2026-10-16, which asked for every claim "in the source's own key words" and every quotation of 15 or more words copied, ran at 2.63 times the source (median; 0.73 to 5.67; 553,000 findings words for 254,927 source words; one unit of 2,058 words became 8,647 findings words, 42 claims items of 78 words on average). The writer therefore carried the source more than twice over with the note: writer contexts ended at 432k to 642k tokens, the write phase was 55 to 68 percent of each task's cost, and the intakes together cost 2.1 times the high estimate (1,170M cache-read by records). And the quoting form changed: the reader paraphrased claims into a short separate Quotations list and the writer, who could quote only from the findings, left 644 of the originals' quotations in the note as the same words unquoted and lost 358 that never reached the findings verbatim (quotation recall 20.4 to 64.0 percent). The owner's ruling of 2026-10-23 adopted the compact form of section 3 (wip/compact-findings/, which re-measures it on two of those sources): the reader writes pointers and key words, the writer pulls the words by pointer, so the findings stop being a second copy and the Claim cells quote the source as the single-agent notes do.

What the second measurement found, and the split write (2026-10-23; wip/compact-findings/REPORT.md sections 5 and 8). The compact form halved the findings (3.79 to 1.50 times the source on one source, 2.58 to 1.27 on the other) and raised coverage (quotation recall 31 to 59 percent and 40 to 76, claims 74 to 88 and 92 to 94), but the write phase cost more, not less (49.4M records against 37.5M on the first source): the findings were about 25k tokens of a 432k writer context, and the rest was the system (50k), the briefs, schema and context pack re-read on every call (about 100k), and the writer's own parts, text and thinking, over 101 calls against 74. Cost is calls times context, so the cuts are to the standing reading and the calls: the write is now two agents, writer A (the metadata table, the claims ledger, the forthrightness audit) and writer B (every other section, from the finished ledger by line search), each reading the schema and the briefs by line range and only the sections its part needs, in parts of about 3,000 words (half the part calls); the ledger's row numbers reach writer B through `ledger-notes.md` and `grep`, not by reading the ledger. The same run found the writer rowing one claim per quoted sentence (474 rows against the original's 177); write.md now defines the proposition and forbids a split by sentence.

## 3. The findings file

`wip/<task>/findings/NNN.md`, one per unit, the fixed form read.md gives (the gate parses it): a head (`unit:` the unit file's own header line, `status: complete`, `reader:`, and `size: over budget: <reason>` when the unit needs the exception below), then the sections `## Summary`, `## Dated events`, `## Claims`, `## Quotations`, `## Questions, declines, walls and stated limits`, `## Names`, `## Documents and works cited`, `## Objects (<the pack's watch name>)`, the pack's actors heading, `## Relations`, optional `## Tensions inside the source`, `## Open threads the unit raises`, `## Nothing to row`. Every item is one line, ` | `-separated fields in a fixed order, opening with the pointer `[u NNN:L]` (or `[u NNN:L-M]`), the unit number and the `cat -n` line of the unit file where the item rests.

**The compact form (2026-10-23).** The findings file is a digest the writer navigates, not a second copy of the source (section 2 gives the measurement that forced it). Each field is a few words, never a sentence:

- a **claim** item is the pointer, the speaker, the topic code, the class, the move, the claim in at most about 20 of the source's own key words, the chain and `loc:` (the Time cell's paragraph, page or stamp), with `attr:` and `note:` only where there is something to say; an item names every proposition its pointer's span holds, separated by `;`, each by its key words (a span of more than three is split into items), because the writer rows from the key words and does not re-read the span;
- a **quotation** item is the pointer and line range, the speaker, `words:` with the span's first four and last four words (`<first four> ... <last four>`; a span of eight words or fewer given whole) and `use:`, never the text; the gate locates the anchors and `ledger quote --words` prints the span between them;
- dated events, questions and declines, names, works, objects, kinds and relations keep their fields, each a few words.

The coverage rules do not change and apply to items, not words: every unit read in full; every claim, event, quotation-worthy span (15 or more words that carry a claim, a description or a document's wording, and shorter ones where the words matter), name, work, object, kind, relation, question and decline present as an item; nothing omitted because it looks minor.

**The size budget is an item allowance** (2026-10-23; the quarter of the first compact form could not be met: wip/compact-findings/REPORT.md section 4, all twelve measured units over it at 1.00 to 1.89, because the coverage rule fixes 43 to 63 items per 1,000 source words and a compact item has a floor of about 20 words). The findings file's words are at most 200 plus 35 per claim item, 30 per question item, 20 per quotation item and 25 per every other item; the gate computes the allowance from the items it counts, so density never puts a unit over, only words per item do. The reader checks it before marking the unit done (`--size`), cuts fields to the form when over and never drops an item. The one exception is declared in a form the gate checks, `size: over budget: <section>: <n> items over <cap> words: <reason>`, for a section whose items cannot be cut to their cap and stay exact (report numbers and URLs; a question whose wording is the point); the gate confirms the named section is the one over. Gate 1 fails an over-budget unit without a valid exception line. Ten of the twelve measured units meet the allowance with no exception.

**The writer pulls the words.** The writer never reads the units whole; it pulls the spans of the rows it is writing with `python3 ledger.py quote --wip wip/<task> u NNN:L-M [u ...]` (several pointers per call), a quotation item's words with `--words "<first four> ... <last four>"`, a unit's quotation items with `--quotations NNN`. Every quotation in the note is copied from that output. The claims row's grain is the proposition, as the single-agent notes row it (write.md defines it: one assertion by one speaker with one class and one move; a sentence that only extends it stays in its row; never a split by sentence or by quotation): one findings item may yield several rows. Where a claim rests on the source's words the Claim cell quotes them, pulled from the pointer, so the note's quoting form matches the single-agent originals. Two owner rules of 2026-10-23 bind the writer (write.md; the document form brief): a source's own citations are verified against and recorded in Works and in the claim's chain, not rowed as the cited work's own claims, unless TASK.md or the run brief names the cited work as a source; and no transformation of the findings or of quotations that no brief states (a writer that builds rows by script keeps the script under the task's wip folder and names it).

The reader is told what the form is for: the writer navigates by the reader's pointers, so a claim, date, quotation, name, document, object, kind or relation that is not an item does not reach the note. Comparison with the database (held rows, first-telling tags, narrative rows, leads, Agreement cells) is the writer's work from the pre-pass files; the reader does not read context.md or any held note, so its context stays the unit in hand.

The form of 2026-10-16 (every claim "in the source's own key words", every quotation of 15 or more words copied in full) is still accepted by the gate, so archived findings still check; new runs use the compact form.

## 4. The gates

`python3 tools/intake_gate.py --findings --wip wip/<task> [--units A-B] [--min-words 4] [--strict] [--mark] [--json] [--status]`: every manifest unit has a findings file; its `unit:` line is the unit's header; status complete; every required section present; a unit with no claims has `## Nothing to row`; every `[u NNN:L]` pointer names this unit and a line inside it; every double-quoted span of four or more words anywhere in the file, and every `## Quotations` item of any length in the older form, occurs verbatim in the unit's text; every compact `## Quotations` item carries its pointer and its anchors occur in order on the pointer's lines (two lines' slack, tools/ledger/quote.py); the findings words against the item allowance (200 + 35 per claim item, 30 per question, 20 per quotation, 25 per other item; over it without a valid exception line `size: over budget: <section>: <n> items over <cap> words: <reason>` a failure, section 3; `--size` prints per unit the words, the allowance, the items per section and the state; `--budget R` keeps the older ratio for archived findings, a warning or with `--strict` a failure); a quotation's pointer is within two lines of the words; a claim's pointer leads to lines holding three content words of the claim (a warning; a failure with `--strict`). Exit 1 names the failing units; the workflow resumes the reader of each failing range with the report (`wip/<task>/gate-findings.md`, which gives the nearest source line for every miss). `--mark` sets `findings written` in the manifest for each passing unit (a fan-out's readers also mark as they go; the gate repairs a mark lost to two readers writing the manifest at once). `--status` is the resume point.

`python3 tools/intake_gate.py --note <note> --wip wip/<task> [--min-words 5] [--para-cites] [--strict] [--json]`: every double-quoted span of five or more words in the note (outside `[BLOCKED:` rows; the note's own section titles excluded) occurs verbatim in some unit of the source; a miss in a section that quotes other notes (Cross-checks, What this settles, What this contests, What it concedes, Echo watch, Who hosts, Open questions, Tracker candidates, Divergences read four ways) or on a line carrying `quoted from: <stem or path>` is a warning, elsewhere a failure; every unit is cited at least once (a `[u NNN` marker; a code of the inventory table whose post the unit holds; an h:mm:ss inside a transcript unit's span; a page inside a page span) or named in the note's line `Units with nothing to row: NNN (reason); ...`; every claims-ledger row carrying `[u NNN:L]` points at lines holding three content words of its Claim cell (stems of four letters or more, stop words out). Exit 1 lists the rows and units; the report is `wip/<task>/gate-note.md`. `--para-cites` also resolves the legacy forms `<code> para. <k>` and `<code> § <heading>` through the inventory table to source lines, with a paragraph of tolerance either side (the dry run below; the writer's count of paragraphs is by eye).

`python3 tools/intake_gate.py --note <note> --wip wip/<task> --quotes-only`: the quotation check alone, for a single-agent note whose rows carry no pointer (the forms measurement of 2026-10-24 runs it over the legacy form's notes); the report is `wip/<task>/gate-note-quotes.md`. The fan-out guard (tools/workflows/guarded-intake.js: resume from wip on a death, bisect-block after the last resume) runs it after an agent's reply when the task names its `note` (2026-10-25), and on failure resumes the agent with the report and the duty to clear it, up to `maxGateResumes` (default 2); where an agent's brief tells it to run the gate itself before replying, the loop is the backstop.

`python3 tools/intake_gate.py --note-parts wip/<task>/parts --wip wip/<task> --units A-B [--nothing-to-row <json>] [--start-row N] [--start-audit M]`: the parts mode, after each unit writer of the unit-writers form (section 6): the `u<NNN>-ledger.md` and `u<NNN>-audit.md` parts of the range, table rows only, checked as the note is: every quotation verbatim in the unit its row's pointer names (the gate names the unit that holds it when another does), every claims row ending with a pointer whose lines hold three content words of its claim, every audit row with a pointer whose lines hold words of its question and answer, the rows numbered on without gaps from the start given (a decimal row between its neighbours), every unit of the range cited or in the writer's `nothing_to_row` list; the JSON line gives `last_row` and `last_audit_row`, from which the next writer starts. The report is `wip/<task>/gate-parts-A-B.md`.

Matching: whitespace, curly quotes, dashes and case are normalised; a blockquote's `> ` markers at a unit line's start are dropped (2026-10-24: a quotation may run across a quoted block's lines, as a video description's does); every quote mark becomes an apostrophe (the note's inner quoting style is not a copying error); inline URL tokens of the held captures are dropped; a bracketed insertion of one or two characters (`[M]y`) keeps its content and a longer one (`[Lisle]`, `[sic]`) is dropped, on the quotation and on the source alike; an ellipsis, or a longer insertion, splits a quotation into segments that must appear in order within 600 characters; a quotation may run across a line or paragraph break. Nothing else is forgiven by this first match.

The caption rule (owner 2026-10-25, after the six-form measurement; wip/quote-tool-adoption/): a quotation the first match does not find is matched a second time by word keys (tools/ledger/quote.py: the words lower-cased with their apostrophes dropped, a number's digit-group commas dropped, the punctuation between words not counted; the source's own bracketed words tried both kept and dropped; a caption's `[hh:mm:ss]` stamp never a word), in the same segments and order. A quotation whose keys are the unit's in order differs by punctuation, capitals or apostrophes only: it is reported as `punctuation only`, listed apart in the report under `## Punctuation only`, counted in the JSON line's `quote_punct`, and is never a failure (a quotation from an unpunctuated caption may add punctuation and capitals but never change, add or drop a word). Any other miss is `changed words`: a failure (a warning in the sections that quote other notes), counted in `quote_fails`, with the nearest window's difference named (`the source has "x" where the quotation has "y"`, `the quotation adds "z"`, `the quotation drops "w"`). A substituted acronym, a dropped article, a shortened phrase inside quotation marks all fail, which is the point. The rule binds gate 2 on a note, `--quotes-only` and the parts mode alike; gate 1 is unchanged. `ledger quote --find` locates a phrase by the same keys, so a phrase the gate would forgive is one `--find` finds.

## 5. The dry run on an archived single-agent run

The archived single-agent run of 2026-10-15 on a batch of web posts on a programme's documents (pair 2 B of section 1: set B of one blog's held posts, 29 posts, 22 units, 52,516 words), against its parts/findings.md and its finished note (463 claims rows). Reports: research/tasks/tool-feedback/intake-staged-dryrun-1016/. The quoted examples in this section are illustrative, on the cases of tools/example-casebook.md; each stands for a real failure of the same kind, and the dry-run reports hold the real ones. The figures are the dry run's.

Gate 1 (`--legacy-findings`, the old single file split by its `## NNN` headings; the section checks do not apply to the old form): 12 of 22 units pass; 13 quotations in 10 units are not verbatim. Every one is a real defect of the kind the gate exists for, and every one would have been caught before the note was written: shorthand in quotation marks (`"cracking in left wall"` for the memo's "cracking in the spillway's left wall"; `"no warning in writing"` for "no warning was ever put in writing"), an acronym substituted inside the quotation (`"The MSB opened an inquiry into the transfers"` where the post says "the Markets Standards Board"; `"the first of three CAC certificates for the wall"` where the source says "Calloway Ashdown Civil"), a dropped article (`"inspector at Authority's Harrowmere office"` for "inspector at the Authority's Harrowmere office"), and paraphrases set in quotation marks (`"Harlowe's knowledge of the Lisle memo during the period in question"`, `"Where there's a memo there's a cover-up"`). None of these reached the note as a misquotation in the end, but only because the single agent re-read the source when it wrote; a writer that never sees the source would have copied them, which is why the gate sits between the two.

Gate 2 (`--para-cites`): 461 quotations checked; 12 not verbatim in source-bound sections, 35 warnings in the sections that quote other notes (29 in Cross-checks); 0 uncited units (every post is reached through the inventory table's codes); 367 of 463 claims rows resolved to source lines from their `Pnn para. k` and `Pnn § heading` cells, 49 of them failing the three-word test (13 with no content word in the window, 14 with one, 22 with two), 96 rows unresolved (a section heading the resolver could not find, or a paragraph number beyond the post's count). The 12 quotation failures are quotations the note takes from somewhere other than the source: held documents and the sibling set's posts cited through two other held notes (`"two of five pages were withheld in full"`, `"the Authority is unaware which inspections the inquiry means"`, `"Vale Water Authority Asset Records Office"`), and one headline that exists in the source only as a URL slug (`"the spillway may simply have been overtopped"`). Under the new form each would carry `quoted from: <stem or path>` and pass as a warning; without the marker the gate cannot tell them from a misquotation, and should not. The 49 line failures are mostly paraphrases that keep none of the source's key words (`Readers should demand nothing less than the records of how much the Authority knew`), and some are the paragraph count differing between the writer's eye and the script's (hence the tolerance); under the new form the row carries the reader's `[u NNN:L]` pointer and the test is exact. The 35 warnings are the expected ones: Cross-checks quoting the other note's row, Echo watch quoting a held telling.

What the dry run shows: the gates are strict enough to catch the shorthand-in-quotes habit and tolerant enough to pass 449 of 461 real quotations (97 percent) in a note written without them in mind; and the only quotations they cannot settle are ones whose provenance the note does not state, which the write brief now makes it state.

## 6. Running it

Before launch, per task (as the batch briefs of 2026-10-15 did): `python3 tools/chunk_source.py <source> --wip wip/<task> [--by post --set B]`; `python3 tools/note_skeleton.py --form <form> --task wip/<task> --note books/<note>.md --source <held>`; `python3 tools/context_pack.py <source> --wip wip/<task> [--min-count 3 --max-rows-per-term 5 --broad 60]`; `python3 tools/timeline_prepass.py wip/<task>/units --wip wip/<task> [--window 1 --year-window 0 --cap 4]`; write `wip/<task>/TASK.md` (the source, the note path, the form, the label, the subjects, the run-specific brief, the held threads, whether adverse) and `wip/<task>/prompt.md`. Take `units` and `words` from the manifest's totals line. Then, before the launch, request keep-awake before launch and hold it until the run's last agent has replied: in the Claude desktop app its keep-awake tool (request_keep_awake, until the session is idle); elsewhere the system's own (macOS `caffeinate -i`, Linux `systemd-inhibit`, Windows the power plan or `powercfg /requestsoverride`). A sleeping computer kills the agent in flight (2026-10-24: a writer died mid-response and the guard's resume cost 4.8M records); a closed lid still sleeps (tools/agent-wip.md item 9). The task below is illustrative (the casebook's *Margin Notes* blog, in the dimensions of the dry run's source); the call's form is exact.

```
Workflow({scriptPath: "tools/workflows/staged-intake.js", args: {
  tasks: [{key: "intake-margin-notes", wip: "wip/intake-margin-notes", note: "books/margin-notes-strand-harrowmere-2021.md",
           form: "adverse-web", label: "Margin Notes set B", units: 22, words: 52516}],
  model: "opus", effort: "high", gateModel: "haiku", maxResumes: 2, maxGateResumes: 2,
  check: false}})
```

That call runs the default form, unit writers (three writers for this source's 52,516 words). Options: `ranges: [[1, 11], [12, 22]]` on a task forces the staged form's reader fan-out; without it a source over `fanoutWords` (default 35,000 words in the staged form) is split into ranges of `readerUnits` units, by default as many units as hold about `fanoutWords` words at the task's own words per unit (a 40,000-word source of 16 units gets two readers of 8); the gate and usage probes run `python3`, or the project's venv interpreter `.ledger/venv/bin/python3` when it exists (`python` in the args overrides); `prompts: {read, write_a, write_unit, check, write_b, finish}` replaces a phase's generated prompt (the phase brief is still read); `sample: false` (in args or on a task) skips the sample phase, never by default, and the result carries `sample_reading: {skipped: "sample false"}`; `sampleThreshold` overrides the pack's `[intake] sample_threshold` (default 3), the count of faults of meaning in the twenty that sends a note to the check phase (tools/ledger/sample.py; the single-agent form, tools/workflows/guarded-intake.js, runs the same phase after its quotation gate and, having no check phase, flags a note at the threshold in its result and log); `splitWrite: false` runs the write as one agent (the form before 2026-10-23, for comparison; default true: writer A then gate 2 then writer B then gate 2); `maxRecords` on a task stops that task alone, between phases and inside the guard, once its cache-read by records passes the number (the other tasks of the run go on); `mergeWriteFinish: true` runs write and finish as one agent (the old tail, for comparison); the two forms (measured on 2026-10-24, wip/forms-measure/REPORT.md; the default from 2026-10-25): `intakeForm: "unit-writers"`, the default (on a task or in the args; a task's `form: "unit-writers"` is taken as the same, as `form: "staged"` is for the other form; otherwise a task's `form` is the skeleton form), has no read phase: unit writers (wip/intake-staged/phases/write-unit.md) run in sequence over unit ranges sized by words, `ceil(words / fanoutWords)` writers with `fanoutWords` 20,000 in this form, so each carries about 10,000 to 20,000 words (`readerUnits` gives the units per writer instead, `unitRanges` forces the ranges, `unitNumbers` gives the manifest's numbers when a unit was left out), each told its range and the rows its ledger and audit start at, each followed by the parts gate and its fix loop; `tools/assemble_parts.py --wip wip/<task> --note <note> --nothing-to-row <json>` (a script, run by a probe) then builds the note from the skeleton and the parts, gate 2 runs on it with a fix loop, and writer B (reading the units where write.md names the findings, and writing the metadata and the audit prose too) and the finish run as in the staged form; `intakeForm: "staged"` runs the read phase, gate 1 and writer A of sections 2 and 3 instead; `check: true` (on a task or in the args; off by default) runs the check phase (wip/intake-staged/phases/check.md) after the ledger stage of either form (gate 2 on writer A's ledger, or on the assembled note) and before writer B, in check.md's order of attention, within `checkBudget` rows when one is given (on a task or in the args; default every row), with gate 2 and its fix loop after it, and hands its unsupported rows and outside matter to writer B and the finisher; `bisectScript` and `phasesDir` as in the other scripts. The script returns per task the phases run, each agent's result head, each gate's summary and report path, and `blocked_passages` collected from every reply; a task stops at the first phase whose agent is dead after the resumes or whose gate still fails after `maxGateResumes` fixes, and says so (`stopped_at`). The gates run as Haiku probes at low effort that execute the command and relay its JSON line; they read nothing else. After the run: `python3 tools/workflow_health.py <run>` as before, `python3 tools/agent_usage.py <run> --by-phase` for the measure, and the integration steps of the standing procedure.

## 7. Measuring the next run: `tools/agent_usage.py`

`python3 tools/agent_usage.py [<run id or dir>] [--by-phase] [--json] [--md]` reads the run folder (journal.jsonl, agent-<id>.jsonl, agent-<id>.meta.json) and prints, per agent and in total: `turns` (assistant records, one per streamed content block: the baseline's measure), `calls` (distinct API calls, each re-reading the whole context), `cache-create` and `cache-read` (summed once per call), `cache-read (records)` (summed over every record, how the baseline's figures were taken), `output (est)` (the transcript's usage.output_tokens is the stream's opening count, so output is estimated as the content blocks' characters divided by four), `context at end` (input plus cache-create plus cache-read of the last call), `reads` (Read calls plus cat/sed/head/tail/less commands), `greps` (Grep calls plus grep/rg commands), `writes` (Write/Edit calls plus heredocs, cp, mv, tee), `wall`, and whether the journal recorded a result. `--by-phase` subtotals by the workflow phase in each agent's meta (Read, Gate 1, Write, Gate 2, Finish, Resume, Probe), which is the comparison that matters for the staged run: the sum of cache-read over all phases against one agent's, and the largest context at end in any phase against 750k to 890k. `--md` prints the table for this page.

## 8. What stays the same

The schema, the standing briefs, the report shape, integrate_report.py and the integration steps, the blocked-output procedure, the wip checkpoints, the pre-pass tools and their settings, the intake policy and the source balance. The owner's three rules bind the staged form as they bind the single-agent form: no token is saved by omitting work, accuracy before speed, Opus at high effort for every agent that reads or writes (the gate probes run a script and relay a line; they judge nothing).
