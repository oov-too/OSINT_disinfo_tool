# Intake policy: balancing the sources (owner, 2026-10-14)

A corpus built a few compilers deep reads the milieu's own voice back to itself: most of its notes come from a handful of compilers and the hosts they draw on, and its claims by source class run to many host and firsthand rows for every programme-record row. In the casebook, a ledger on the Harrowmere spillway built from *The Long Table*'s 2022 series and the *Harbour Talks* interview it draws on would hold dozens of host and firsthand rows about the Lisle memo for every row citing the memo itself, and most of what it dates before the catalyst (the *Ostley Gazette* story of 9 October 2017) would have no telling held from before that date. The first-utterance tests (tools/first_utterance.py) measure that last gap: the share of the narrative pages' pre-catalyst nodes that have any held telling from before the catalyst. The counts that moved the owner to these rules (notes per compiler, rows per source class, the first-utterance share) are this project's own and live in the active pack ({{pack.compilers}}; the catalyst, {{pack.catalysts}}). These six rules bind every intake from this date. The owner's own practice from here: no more intake from the channels the corpus already holds in abundance (the counts tools/source_balance.py reports) unless a note is an earliest telling, a divergence or a document.

## The rules

1. **Earliest telling first.** No later telling of a claim enters without a search for its earliest public telling, and the earliest enters with it (or is queued in research/early-tellings-review.md with the reason it could not be had). The intake report records the search (`earliest_telling_search`): what was looked for, where (Wayback, publish dates, Google Books, newspaper archives such as the *Ostley Gazette*'s, an agency's own reading rooms and releases, an inquiry's exhibit lists, the dated postings of archive sites; the archives the active pack searches are {{pack.archives}}), and what was found. Every row the new note dates earlier than a held telling carries the tag `[first telling: FU-n]` in its Note cell, where FU-n is the cluster in research/first-utterance-register.md (a case-insensitive search for the term in research/first-utterance-register.md with the Grep tool finds it; a claim with no cluster is reported as `new: <claim>`). The report's `first_telling_rows` lists them: `{"cluster": "FU-n" | "new: <claim>", "row": "[note kind n]", "date": "YYYY-MM-DD", "venue": "...", "event": "<event date, for a new cluster>"}`; the orchestrator applies it with `python3 tools/first_utterance.py --apply <report>`, which also refreshes the register. The register is maintained at intake and only read by the narrative process.
2. **Agency documents when a carrier cites one.** If a telling rests on a document, the document comes in, from the agency's own release where one exists (in the casebook: the Vale Water Authority's 2018 review, *Report on the Harrowmere Event*, and the Authority's disclosures to the Harrowmere Inquiry as numbered exhibits; the Markets Standards Board's published decisions; the Joint Trade Panel's public rulings; the agency releases and reading rooms the active pack names are {{pack.archives}}), as its own note in the document form.
3. **One adverse source per circle per era.** For every circle the relations register names, and every era the synchrony pages divide, the corpus holds at least one dated adverse telling: in the casebook, *Margin Notes* (Ivo Strand's engineering blog, sceptical of the cover-up reading) for the press circle in the era 2017 onward, and, for the engineering circle in the era 2009 to 2016, a sceptical engineering review of the 2009 design; the circles and the adverse sources named for each in the active pack are {{pack.circles}}. An adverse source is intaken under the same schema and the same forthrightness audit as any other; adversity is a class, not a discount.
4. **A new carrier must add a root, not a mouth.** A relay of a held root enters only as an earliest telling (rule 1) or as a divergence (the divergence rule). The intake report says which roots the note adds (`roots_added`) and which held roots it merely relays.
5. **Leaks carry their chain.** A leaked document is a hearsay row with the leaker (named or anonymous) as its root, `source_class: hearsay` with `chain: 1: <leaker> (leak)`, never `program-record`, until an agency release matches it; in the casebook the Corvane files (leaked to the press in 2019 by an anonymous source) are the standing example: the Larkspur spreadsheet held in them is cited `source_class: hearsay` with `chain: 1: anonymous source (leak)` until the Markets Standards Board or a court releases a matching copy, and the Dorr cables (leaked in 2015) are held the same way. The active pack names its own standing example ({{pack.exemplars}}).
6. **A quota.** For every milieu-compiler video intaken, one agency document and one adverse source, until the source classes are closer to balance than the host rows against programme-record rows that tools/source_balance.py reported on the date of this policy (this project's baseline ratio is pack data, {{pack.compilers}}). tools/source_balance.py reports the balance and the quota ledger since this date; the orchestrator runs it before every intake launch and names the deficit in the launch note.
7. **Cited-source scope.** (owner 2026-10-23) An intake rows what its source says, including what the source says a cited work says (class `read`, chain naming the work). The source's own citations are verified against and recorded in Works, in the chain and as first-telling or lead candidates; they are never rowed as the cited work's own claims inside the citing note (the round trip's note on one web article rowed 818 claims that way, where the single-agent note held 92 rows; wip/platform-phase6a/REPORT.md finding 18). A cited work that matters comes in as its own intake under rule 2, queued in `chain_leads`, unless the task names it as a source of the intake.

## Source kinds

Every note carries a kind in research/source-kinds.md (the balance tool classifies by stem pattern and the file overrides): `compiler` (a compiler's own narrative video or post: in the casebook, *The Long Table*'s 2022 series; the active pack's compilers and the stem patterns that sort them are {{pack.compilers}}), `host` (an interview or podcast carrying a guest), `witness` (a carrier speaking for themselves outside a compiler's frame: a talk, a book, a post), `document` (an agency or court record, a contemporaneous paper, a diary), `adverse` (a sceptical or opposed telling), `earliest-telling` (an intake made under rule 1). The intake report's `source_kind` key names it.

## Enforcement

- The intake briefs' standing rules cite this file; tools/report_check.py requires `source_kind`, `earliest_telling_search` and `roots_added` in every intake report from this date.
- tools/source_balance.py prints the balance and the quota ledger; a launch that would widen the imbalance is noted as such in the run log before it runs.
- tools/first_utterance.py is re-run after every intake batch and its report committed.
