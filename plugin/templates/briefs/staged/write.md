# Phase: write (staged intake, step 2 of 3; two writers)

Core template (platform phase 4, 2026-10-22; the compact findings form of 2026-10-23; the split write of 2026-10-23; from wip/intake-staged/phases/write.md of 2026-10-23), rendered into `wip/<run>/briefs/staged/write.md`.

| Slot | What it holds | Core default |
|---|---|---|
| `{{pack.free_sections.document}}`, `{{pack.free_sections.video}}` | the pack's own free sections writer B builds from the findings | none |
| `{{pack.blocked_precedent}}` | the precedent named for the pointer row | the pointer-row form of intake-standing.md's blocked-output paragraph |

The compact findings form (2026-10-23): the findings carry pointers and key words, and you pull the source's words by pointer. The split write (2026-10-23, after the re-measure, wip/compact-findings/REPORT.md section 5: the findings were never the writer's large term; its standing reading, its own output and its number of calls are): the note is written by two agents in turn, **writer A, the ledger** (the metadata table, the claims ledger, the forthrightness audit) and **writer B, the sections** (everything else), each reading only what its part needs. The workflow names your role in its prompt; read the shared rules, then your role's section, and nothing of the other role's reading.

You write the note in the house schema from the findings files and the pre-pass files. **You never read the source whole**: not `wip/<task>/units/` with the Read tool or a print of the file, not the held copy or the transcript, not the original URL. The findings files, `wip/<task>/findings/NNN.md`, are the digest you navigate: every claim, event, name, document, object, kind, relation, question and decline of the source is an item there, with the pointer `[u NNN:L]` or `[u NNN:L-M]` to the unit lines it rests on. The source's own words you pull by those pointers with `ledger quote`, and nothing else of the source enters your context. If a findings file lacks something a row needs, say so in your reply's `notes` and in the row's Note cell (`findings gap: unit NNN`), and do not guess it.

## Shared rules (both writers)

**Standing reading, by section, never in full.** Your context is what makes the write cost what it costs (the re-measure: about 100k tokens of briefs, schema and context pack carried on every one of about 100 calls), so you read the standing briefs by heading and line range (the headings with their lines, then the Read tool on the range) and only the sections your role lists below. tools/agent-wip.md in full (it is short). `wip/<task>/TASK.md` in full. Nothing of the schema, the standing brief, the context pack or an exemplar beyond the sections named for your role.

**Pulling the source's words.**

- `ledger quote --wip wip/<task> u NNN:L-M [u NNN:L-M ...]` prints each pointer's unit lines verbatim under the unit's header. Pull the spans of the rows you are about to write in one or two calls per part (several pointers per call), not one call per row, and do not pull a span twice.
- `ledger quote --wip wip/<task> --quotations NNN` prints every Quotations item of findings/NNN.md with the exact words its anchors locate, and `ledger quote --wip wip/<task> u NNN:L-M --words "<first four> ... <last four>"` one item's words.
- A quotation in the note is copied from that output, never retyped from memory and never from the findings' key words: curly quotes, dashes and spelling as printed; shortened only by `...` (an omission) and `[brackets]` (an insertion), never by cutting words inside the quotation marks. An OCR error, a line-break hyphen or a misspelling in the printed span is copied as printed and marked: `[sic]` after the word, or the correction in brackets after it; the gates drop bracketed insertions when they match, so the mark costs nothing and a silent correction fails. A pointer the command refuses (exit 1) is a findings defect: say so in `notes` and row the item without the quotation.

**Parts.** You write with the Write tool or Python in parts of about 3,000 words under `wip/<task>/parts/<role>NN.md` (`a01.md`, `b01.md`), in the order the skeleton gives for your sections. After each part, as its own call, `ledger extract verbatim wip/<task>/parts/<role>NN.md` (never chained to the write: the check exits non-zero on a copied span of 40 or more words, and a chained call then shows as a failed write). Before each part, copy the findings file you are rowing to `wip/<task>/current-unit.md` by the copy operation and name it in progress.md. Every call costs your whole context again, so a part is written in one call, checked in one call, and nothing is printed back that you already have. A script you run prints one line (what it wrote, where, the counts), never the text it built; read a part back only by a line search for what you need to check.

**Rows carry their unit line.** Every claims-ledger row's Note cell ends with the pointer of the findings item it comes from, `[u NNN:L]` (or `[u NNN:L-M]`), after everything else in the cell; the Time cell keeps the form's own convention (the post code and paragraph `P04 para. 5` from the item's `loc:`, the page, the h:mm:ss). Audit rows, timeline rows, object-watch rows and notable quotes carry the same pointer at the end of their last cell or after the quote's attribution. A quotation the note takes from a held note or document rather than from this source carries `quoted from: <note stem or held path>` in the same cell. The gate (`ledger extract gate --note <note> --wip wip/<task>`) checks every quotation of the note against the source units, every unit for a citation, and every claims row's pointer against the words at that line; it runs after each writer replies, and the workflow sends the writer its report to fix if it fails, so run it yourself before replying and fix what it names.

**Cited sources (owner's rule, 2026-10-23; the intake policy's rule 7).** The ledger rows what this source says, including what it says a cited work says (class `read`, the chain naming the work). A source's own citations (the articles, documents, posts and books it cites, quotes or links) are verified against and recorded: in Works, with what the source takes from each and whether the cited work bears it out where it is held, in the claim's chain, and as first-telling or lead candidates. They are never rowed as the cited work's own claims, timeline or audit. A cited work that matters comes in as its own intake, queued in `chain_leads`, unless TASK.md or the run brief names it as a source of this intake.

**Nothing from outside the source.** No citation, date, name, figure or case reference from your own knowledge enters a row, labelled unverified or not; what the source does not give is an open question or a lead, never a cell. The comparison with the database comes only from context.md, timeline-candidates.md and the held rows the tools return, and it reaches a row only as a chain hop, a `[first telling: FU-n]` tag or a `[div: ...]` tag: a held date, figure or name the span lacks never enters a Claim or Note cell.

**No transformation no brief states (owner's rule, 2026-10-23).** Every findings item reaches the note as the briefs say; nothing is filtered, ranked or cut by a rule of your own: no "notable items only", no quotation cut to a length inside its quotation marks (shortening is by `...`, as above), no items merged or dropped to fit a size. A writer that generates rows by script keeps the script under `wip/<task>/` and names it, with what it does, in its reply's `notes`.

**Mechanical parts are generated, not typed** (intake-standing.md, "Generated parts"): the derivable metadata keys, the document inventory, the audit counts line and the cross-check table come from `ledger extract fill` in the finish phase; leave the skeleton's markers in place. The Cross-checks section stays as the skeleton gives it (the empty table and the marker) for the finish phase.

## Writer A: the ledger

**What you write.** The metadata table as the skeleton gives it (the keys you can fill from TASK.md and the findings' Summary items; the markers left for the fill step), the line `Units with nothing to row: NNN (<the reason>); MMM (<the reason>)` directly above the `## Claims ledger` heading when any unit's findings file says `## Nothing to row` (every other unit has at least one row pointing at it), the `## Claims ledger` in full, and the `## Forthrightness audit` in full (its rows, its prose, the counts marker left for the fill step). Nothing else: no People, Works, timeline, quotes or free sections, which writer B builds from your finished ledger.

**What you read, and only this.** tools/schema.md sections 1 (the metadata table), 2 (the claims ledger), 3 (the forthrightness audit), "Parser rules worth knowing" and "Rules for agents", with the pack's clauses to them, by line range. intake-standing.md: its standing rules, report keys, blocked-output and units sections; the form brief TASK.md names (intake-document.md or intake-video.md): its metadata conventions and report keys; intake-adverse.md when TASK.md says adverse (it is short: in full); operations.md for the commands. `wip/<task>/skeleton.md`: the metadata table and the ledger and audit sections, by line range. `wip/<task>/context.md`: sections 1 (what the pre-pass extracted), 2 (held rows by name, date, identifier and URL, with 2e) and 3 (first-utterance clusters), for the chain, the `[first telling: FU-n]` tags and the `[div: ...]` tags on rows that diverge from held rows; open a held note only for a row context.md cites. Every `wip/<task>/findings/NNN.md` in manifest order, in full. An exemplar's ledger section only, by the range the skeleton's comment gives. Not `timeline-candidates.md`, not context.md sections 4 and 5, not the rest of the schema or the briefs.

**The claims ledger's grain is the proposition.** A proposition is one assertion by one speaker with one class and one move: what is asserted, of whom or what, when, and on what basis. A sentence that only extends the same assertion (an example, a restatement, a qualifier, the second clause of the same thing, the speaker's own gloss on it) stays in its row; a new assertion (a different fact, date, figure, object, actor or basis) is a new row. One findings item yields one row per proposition its claim field names (the `;`-separated key words), and a row may quote several sentences while one sentence may hold two rows. A `;` segment that the single-agent notes would row alone (a date, a figure, a second actor, a stated reason with its own basis) is its own row; only an example, a restatement or the speaker's gloss stays in the row before. Two adjacent findings items that are fragments of one proposition (one list, one sentence cut at a page or chunk boundary) become one row with both pointers cited; that join is not a merge under the no-transformation rule. The `;`-separated key words name the parts of a claim and are not a row count: most items are one proposition and yield one row; split only where a part is a date, a figure, a second actor or a stated reason with its own basis. **Never split by sentence and never split by quotation**: the re-measure's document ledger ran to 474 rows against the original's 177 by rowing one claim per quoted sentence, and its precision fell to 35 percent. A rule's or a document's numbered sub-clauses are one row where they jointly state one requirement, finding or event, and separate rows where each asserts a distinct obligation, fact, actor or date; the row names the sub-clause numbers it covers in its Time cell (owner ruling by the orchestrator, 2026-10-26). A ledger past about 30 rows per 1,000 source words is almost always split by sentence: stop and re-read this paragraph before the next part.

**Claim with quote.** Where a claim rests on the source's words (what a speaker says, what a document states), the Claim cell quotes those words, pulled from the item's pointer: the speaker's or the document's own wording inside quotation marks with the cell's paraphrase around it, as the single-agent notes do (`Says the left wall "was poured at night in August 2009" after the weather held the work back`). Quote the words that carry the claim, at the length the claim needs (a phrase, a sentence; `...` for a cut); a row with no wording worth quoting (an inference, a host's framing) carries none. A quotation may begin or end mid-sentence only where the cut drops nothing that limits what is kept (a restrictive clause, a qualifier, a negative, a condition); where it would, quote through the limiting words or mark the cut with `...` and give the limit outside the marks. A quotation from an unpunctuated caption may add punctuation and capitals but never changes, adds or drops a word. Every `firsthand` row names the act of seeing, doing, measuring or receiving the span states; a flat statement by a document's author or an organisation's member about anything they did not do is `host` (schema rules cr, ct). Where you doubt a findings item's class, pull the span and decide; never write `as the findings read it` in a Note. The audit rows that turn on wording pull their pointer likewise. A claims row an audit row rests on ends its Note cell, before the pointer, with `Audit N`, that audit row's number: the charts and the topic panels link the two through it, and `(audit)` without the number links nothing.

**The paraphrase around a quotation says no more than the span.** The gate checks only the words inside quotation marks, so the paraphrase outside them is the one place a row can reverse, extend or soften what the source says without any check catching it (the pilot's RPM note, row 22: a paraphrase reversed a stated limit the key words had right). Write the paraphrase from the pulled span, not from the key words; where the sense turns on a word, quote the word. The chain's set phrases are the schema's and are not improvised: a hearsay row about a named or anonymous party with no teller carries `without saying how it reached him` in the attribution (rule o), a named author with an unnamed work carries `author named, work not named` and `(document, unnamed)` (rule ce), and the chain prefix ends with its period before anything else in the cell, the pointer included (rule j: `chain: 1: Castleton (firsthand). <the rest> [u 001:303]`).

**When you finish.** `ledger extract gate --note <note> --wip wip/<task>` passes on the note as assembled (the skeleton with your metadata, the nothing-to-row line, the ledger and the audit in place, every other section left as the skeleton gives it, `Status: in progress`; assemble by Python, concatenating the skeleton's other sections unchanged). Write `wip/<task>/ledger-notes.md` for writer B: one line per unit naming the rows it holds (`unit 003: rows 41 to 58`), the speakers and the row ranges of each; one line per dated event of the findings naming every claims row that carries it or a figure, amount or name tied to it (`1949 fall: rows 302, 325 to 327`), so writer B's timeline misses nothing the ledger holds; and anything B must know that no findings file carries. `[div: ...]` tags carry `party=` as schema section 10 gives it. `report.json` holds `claims_rows`, `first_telling_rows` (the keys `cluster`, `row`, `date`, `venue`, `event`), `blocked_passages` and `narrative_rows_addressed` as far as the ledger settles them. Reply with the structured fields: `file`, `words`, `claims_rows`, `parts`, `verbatim_check` ("0 spans in every part", or what remains and why), `blocked_passages` (`[]` when none), `notes` (with any script you used to build rows, its path and what it does).

## Writer B: the sections

**What you write.** Every section of the skeleton except the three writer A wrote and the Cross-checks placeholder, in skeleton order: the one-paragraph summary and the form's reading sections (Key claims and ideas; the form's grids; What this settles, What this contests, What it concedes, Who hosts, Echo watch of the adverse and text forms), `## People`, `## Works`, the object watch, `## Notable quotes`, `## Tracker candidates`, `## Dated timeline` (every Agreement cell ruled from `timeline-candidates.md`), `## Open questions`, and the pack's free sections ({{pack.free_sections.document}}; for a recording {{pack.free_sections.video}}). You do not change a ledger or audit row; a defect you find in one goes in `notes` with the row number, and the finisher corrects it.

**What you read, and only this.** tools/schema.md sections 4 to 9, 11 and 12, with the pack's clauses to them, by line range. intake-standing.md: its standing rules, the narratives section, the blocked-output and timeline pre-pass sections; the form brief's free-section list (intake-video.md for a recording); intake-adverse.md when TASK.md says adverse; operations.md for the commands. `wip/<task>/skeleton.md` in full. `wip/<task>/ledger-notes.md` (writer A's map of the ledger). `wip/<task>/context.md`: sections 1, 3 (first-utterance clusters, for the timeline's first-telling tags), 4 (narrative rows) and 5 (leads); section 2 only by a search for a name or date you need. `wip/<task>/timeline-candidates.md` in full. The findings files by section, never whole: their `## Summary`, `## Dated events`, `## Names`, `## Documents and works cited`, the object-watch heading, the actors heading, `## Relations`, `## Tensions inside the source` and `## Open threads` sections, printed for every unit in manifest order by one Python call that keeps those headings and drops the rest; the Quotations items through `ledger quote --wip wip/<task> --quotations NNN`; the Claims items never (they reach you through the ledger by a line search). **The ledger and the audit never in full**: a row you cite (a People row's "rows 3, 17", a timeline row's claim, a notable quote's row) is found by a line search on the note for the speaker, the date or the pointer, and read as the lines the search returns; the audit's counts you take from writer A's ledger-notes.md. An exemplar by section only, the range the skeleton's comment gives.

**Rows and quotes.** People, Works, object-watch, actor and relations rows come from the findings' Names, Documents, Objects, actor and Relations items, each carrying its pointer; an actor row's posture is toward the referent ({{pack.referent}}), by whose telling; a notable quote and a timeline row's "date as stated" pull their pointer or their Quotations item (`--quotations NNN` for a unit's quotation items at once). The cross-check candidates you see in context.md may be listed in `wip/<task>/xc-notes.md` for the finisher, with the row on each side you read.

**When you finish.** The note is assembled by Python: writer A's sections unchanged, yours in their skeleton places, `Status: in progress`. `ledger prepare skeleton --check <note> --form <form>` passes (the Cross-checks placeholder and the Status key are allowed); `ledger extract gate --note <note> --wip wip/<task>` passes; `report.json` now holds every key the standing and form briefs list that you can fill (timeline_rows, the pack's actor rows, relations, chain_leads, subject_rows, register_new, with writer A's keys kept; the finisher fills the rest). Reply with the structured fields: `file`, `words`, `claims_rows` (writer A's count, unchanged), `parts`, `verbatim_check`, `blocked_passages`, `notes` (with any script you used, its path and what it does, and any ledger or audit row you found defective).
