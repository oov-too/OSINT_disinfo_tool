# Phase: write-unit (the unit-writers form, 2026-10-25)

Core template (platform, 2026-10-25; from wip/intake-staged/phases/write-unit.md of 2026-10-25), rendered into `wip/<run>/briefs/staged/write-unit.md`. The unit-writers form is the shipped default intake (the six-form measurement of 2026-10-24: exact quotation, the fewest faults of meaning, 75 percent of the staged form's cost).

| Slot | What it holds | Core default |
|---|---|---|
| `{{pack.topics}}` | the closed topic codes of a claims row | `other` only |
| `{{pack.basis_vocabulary}}` | the grounds a stated decline may give | none |
| `{{pack.blocked_precedent}}` | the precedent named for the pointer row | the pointer-row form of intake-standing.md's blocked-output paragraph |
| `{{pack.referent}}` | the referent an actor's posture is toward | none |

The unit-writers form replaces the read phase and writer A of the staged intake with one agent per unit range: **you write the claims-ledger rows and the forthrightness-audit rows for your units with the unit itself in your context**, no findings digest between you and the source. The measurements that led here (wip/platform-phase6a/REPORT.md; wip/compact-findings/REPORT.md; wip/split-write/REPORT.md; the pilot and legacy readings of 2026-10-23/24): the single-agent form, which reads the source whole, made no fault of meaning in 80 sampled rows; the staged form's faults of meaning (7.7 percent) entered at the hand-off from the reader's digest to the writer. This form keeps the source in the writer's context, one unit at a time, and keeps the staged form's gates. Writer B (write.md, "Writer B: the sections") then builds every other section from your finished ledger, and the finish phase runs as now.

## What you read, and only this

`wip/<task>/TASK.md` in full; tools/agent-wip.md in full. tools/schema.md sections 1, 2, 3, "Parser rules worth knowing" and "Rules for agents", by line range (the headings with their lines, then the Read tool on the range). The rendered standing brief's standing rules, report keys, blocked-output and units sections (`intake-standing.md`, or in the first project intake-standing.md) and the form brief TASK.md names (`intake-document.md`, `intake-video.md`; in the first project intake-video.md for a video), by section; the adverse brief when TASK.md says adverse. `wip/<task>/skeleton.md`: the ledger and audit sections only. `wip/<task>/context.md`: sections 1, 2 and 3 (held rows by name, date, identifier and URL; first-utterance clusters), for the chain, the `[first telling: FU-n]` tags and the `[div: ...]` tags only; a held date, figure or name that the unit lacks goes to ledger-notes.md for writer B, never into a claims row's Note or Claim; open a held note only for a row context.md cites. Your units, one at a time: `wip/<task>/units/NNN.txt` with the Read tool (line 1 is the header), copied to `wip/<task>/current-unit.md` before you row it. Nothing else: not the other units, not timeline-candidates.md, not the rest of the schema or the brief. Your prompt names your unit range and the row number your ledger starts at.

## What you write

For each unit in order: its claims-ledger rows and its forthrightness-audit rows, in the schema's row forms, to `wip/<task>/parts/u<NNN>-ledger.md` and `wip/<task>/parts/u<NNN>-audit.md` (table rows only, no headings), with the Write tool or Python, one unit per call, `ledger extract verbatim <part>` on each part as its own call after it is written. Row numbers run on from the start your prompt gives, across your units, without gaps; the audit rows likewise. Then `wip/<task>/ledger-notes.md` for writer B (one line per unit: its rows; one line per dated event naming every row that carries it or a figure, amount or name tied to it; anything B must know), appended if an earlier unit writer began it.

**Who writes what, in this form.** The metadata table, the audit's prose subsections and the report keys `claims_rows`, `first_telling_rows` and `narrative_rows_addressed` are writer B's; you write the rows. ledger-notes.md therefore names, for each unit, the names, works, objects, relations and dated events writer B will need, with their lines.

**Every row carries its pointer.** The Note cell of every claims row ends, after everything else, with `[u NNN:L]` or `[u NNN:L-M]`, the unit and the `cat -n` lines the row rests on; audit rows carry the same at the end of their last cell. Gate 2 checks every pointer against the words at those lines, every quotation against the unit, and every unit for a citation; a unit that yields no claim is named in your reply's `nothing_to_row` with its reason, and writer B puts the `Units with nothing to row:` line in the note.

**The claims ledger's grain is the proposition** (write.md's definition, which binds here): one assertion by one speaker with one class and one move; a sentence that only extends it stays in its row; a new fact, date, figure, actor or basis is a new row; never a split by sentence or by quotation; a `;` of the source's own list that the single-agent notes would row alone is its own row. A rule's or a document's numbered sub-clauses are one row where they jointly state one requirement, finding or event, and separate rows where each asserts a distinct obligation, fact, actor or date; the row names the sub-clause numbers it covers in its Time cell (owner ruling by the orchestrator, 2026-10-26). The `;`-separated key words of a findings item, where you have one, name the parts of a claim and are not a row count: most items are one proposition and yield one row; split only where a part is a date, a figure, a second actor or a stated reason with its own basis. A ledger past about 30 rows per 1,000 source words is almost always split by sentence.

**Claim with quote, by tool.** Where a claim rests on the source's words, the Claim cell quotes them, and the words are pasted from `ledger quote --wip wip/<task> u NNN:L-M` (the lines by pointer) or `ledger quote --wip wip/<task> --find "<a few of the words>"` (the exact span and its pointer, located by word), never typed from the unit in your context: curly quotes, dashes and spelling as printed, `...` for a cut, `[brackets]` for an insertion, `[sic]` or a bracketed correction for an OCR error or a caption garble, never a silent repair inside the quotation marks. A quotation from an unpunctuated caption may add punctuation and capitals but never changes, adds or drops a word (the gate forgives exactly that). Where the scan cuts a word or a sentence, the paraphrase does not finish it: write `[cut]` or `(the line is garbled)`, and a completion you are sure of goes in square brackets inside the quotation (`instr[ument]`), never as plain paraphrase. A quotation never ends before a clause that limits what is kept (a restrictive clause, a qualifier, a negative, a condition: as, unless, but, if, which, provided): quote through the limiting words, or cut with `...` and give the limit outside the marks. A hedge, a bound or a qualifier in the passage (appear, seem, suggested, alleged, earliest, only, unless, may, as far as, as practiced) goes inside the quotation marks, never into the paraphrase alone. Where a caption line carries a short assent or a second-person aside inside the other speaker's turn (`oh yeah`, `I know you've got`), do not quote it into that speaker's row; if you keep it, say `speaker inferred` in the Note. The paraphrase around the quotation says no more than the passage: nothing from outside the source enters a row, labelled or not; what the unit does not give is an open question for writer B's section, named in ledger-notes.md.

**`firsthand` is an act.** A row is `firsthand` only where the speaker or the document's author says, in the passage, that they saw, did, measured, interviewed or received it themselves; a document's flat statement of fact about any other party, time, practice or event, with no source named, is `host` with `chain: 1: <issuer> (unsourced)` and, in a document, move `closed-subtle`; with a source named, `read`; a member's flat statement about their organisation's holdings, with no word that they saw or handled them, is `host` (schema rule ct). The author of a report or reply is not a witness of what it reports. Every `firsthand` row you write names, in its Claim or Note, the act. In a span with more than one actor, the paraphrase names its actor and never leans on a pronoun; a second actor's act or words are their own row; what a speaker was told or what counsel reads out is `hearsay` or `read` chained to its source as the span gives it, and an unnamed teller stays unnamed.

**Chain, class and move** as schema section 2 and 3 give them: the `attribution: ...; chain: n: ...` prefix on every non-firsthand row with the set phrases (rules o, ce, j), `[div: ...]` tags with `party=`, a document's hedged risk or option statement `host` with move `n/a` (rule cr), the question and answer for an interview, the monologue mapping for a document. A claims row an audit row rests on ends its Note, before the pointer, with `Audit N`.

**Cited sources (rule 7 of tools/intake-policy.md)** and **no transformation no brief states** bind as write.md gives them.

## When you finish your range

`ledger extract gate --note-parts wip/<task>/parts --wip wip/<task> --units <first>-<last>` passes (the gate's parts mode: every quotation of your parts verbatim in its unit, every pointer holding its row's words, every unit of your range cited or named in nothing_to_row); clear every failure it lists, by correcting the words to the source's or marking the repair, and never reply with a quotation failure open. progress.md's last line is `write-unit complete: units <first> to <last>, rows <a> to <b>`. Reply with the structured fields: `units` ("NNN-MMM"), `rows` ("a-b"), `audit_rows` ("a-b"), `parts` (the files), `verbatim_check`, `nothing_to_row` (a list of {unit, reason}, `[]` when none), `blocked_passages` (`[]` when none), `notes`.

## WIP, resume and blocked output (standing)

tools/agent-wip.md binds: progress.md from the first minute naming the unit reached and the next; parts saved per unit; on a resume continue from the last unit whose parts exist, never from scratch. The blocked-output paragraph of intake-standing.md's blocked-output paragraph binds: a passage refused twice is bisected, captured under `wip/<task>/blocked/<n>.md`, and entered as the pointer row ({{pack.blocked_precedent}}) at its place in your ledger. Never pass a unit through your own output beyond the quotations the rows carry; copy files by shell.
